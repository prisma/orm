## New problems, ranked

### Missing advice

1. **Medium. A new discriminator next to an old one the contract keeps is no longer refused at plan time, and the stop that replaces the refusal does not name `--backfill`.** Reproduce (`wip/qa-pr2/r6k9`, `r6k9d`, source `t9k`): `Task` has discriminator `type` and variants `bug` and `epic`; the new contract adds required `kind`, makes it the discriminator, and keeps `type`. Documents hold `type` only. `prisma migration plan --name step --from <hash>` plans one operation (the validator change) with no question. `prisma db migrate` (and `prisma db update`) then stop with "Operation validator.tasks.update stopped: 2 documents … That document has no "kind". … change them with a dataTransform in migration.ts, or in mongosh with an update command that sets bypassDocumentValidation: true …". In round 5 the plan was refused and offered `--backfill Task.kind`. The spec and the subsystem doc now say the document check stops it "with `--backfill Task.type` named for `migration plan`", and for a model's first discriminator the stop does say "This validator change makes "type" required. … To give "type" values you choose, plan the change with migration plan --backfill Task.type." For this case it does not. No data is lost: the mongosh route worked (`$set: { kind: '$type' }` with `bypassDocumentValidation`, then `db update` applied), and `--backfill Task.kind` under `migration plan` still works (`r6k9b`). Should be: the stop says "This validator change makes "kind" required" and names `migration plan --backfill Task.kind`, as it does for a first discriminator.

### Wrong advice

2. **Low. The first-discriminator stop's `--backfill` advice leaves out two steps under `db migrate`, and `--from` everywhere.** Reproduce (`r6fg`): `Task` without a discriminator, two documents; the new contract adds `type` and variants. `migration plan` plans one validator change; `db migrate` stops with "To give "type" values you choose, plan the change with migration plan --backfill Task.type." Followed literally: in a project with migrations and no `db` ref, `migration plan --backfill Task.type` fails with `MIGRATION.PLAN_ORIGIN_UNKNOWN`. With `--from <hash>` it plans a second migration between the same two contracts, and `db migrate` still runs the first one and stops again (`migration status` shows both, the old one pending). It applied only after I deleted the first migration. In a project that only uses `db update` (`r6ff`) the advice works as written: it plans a baseline plus the migration. Should be: under `db migrate`, say to delete the migration that stopped, and give `--from <hash>` as the `db update --backfill` refusal already does.
3. **Low. With the base renamed `Task` to `Job` and no `--rename Task:Job`, the discriminator refusal offers a rename that does not resolve yet.** Reproduce (`r6jb`): source `t9` with `Task` renamed to `Job` (`@@map("tasks")` kept). `migration plan --from <hash>` with no statement: "The documents hold their variant in "Job.type", which the plan removes: add --rename Job.type:Job.kind". Followed: "Cannot resolve statement "--rename Job.type:Job.kind" … the origin has no model "Job" and no earlier statement renames a model to it. → Rename the origin model to "Job" with its own --rename statement before this one". Adding `--rename Task:Job` then works (`r6jc`, `r6jd`). Two steps, nothing written, no data lost. Should be: when no statement renames the model, offer `--rename Task:Job --rename Job.type:Job.kind` together.

### Unclear text

4. **Low. Under `db update`, the plan-wide refusal and the overlap refusals still speak of plans and migrations.** Round 5 point 3(a) was fixed only in the discriminator refusals. Reproduce (`r6u1z`, source `u1b`): `db update --rename User.nick:User.alias` says "To keep their data, plan again with: --rename User.name:User.fullName. … make the change in two migrations: first make the rename and apply it, without the new required field; then add the new field." With both renames: "Make the change in two migrations: first rename the keys or fields to names nothing else uses …". A `db update` user runs `db update` twice; the meaning is clear and the route works (round 5 `k3`, round 6 `r6k3`). Should be: "run the command again with" and "in two runs" under `db update`, as the enum questions now say.
